\begin{abstract}
On a single RTX~4080 Laptop GPU, we pin down a training pathology in FiLM-style
regime conditioning of a Mamba selective scan. The conditioner is a per-block
channel-wise affine on the block input, meant to steer the input-dependent
parameters $\Delta,\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}$. Under joint training this modulation is
a \textbf{decisive null}. It collapses to identity on the \emph{weakly-decodable} regime
axes, which we tested with the substrate of a financial world model. The cause is a \emph{gauge} symmetry. An input-side affine's scale folds exactly into the block's own $W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$
projections, and its shift decays as an unrewarded direction. As a result, the optimizer slides the modulation back to identity. The decay follows a dataset-invariant signature ($\tau\approx7000$, $R^2>0.999$) on FI-2010 and Kaggle crypto. It is architecture-general by the algebra of the fold but not yet shown at FM scale. An output-side placement is absorbed the same way, while a language-model control bounds the
result by engaging the modulation only when the regime is strongly decodable. These results are separately confirmed on Polymarket, a structurally different market, thereby demonstrating the null is \emph{overdetermined}. We
establish these results with an exact bit-identical paired control, forced-active router-supervised escalations, a weight-decay ablation, a toy reference-scan, and a char-Mamba language-model control. The financial datasets are only a substrate to test the null.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Selective state-space models (Mamba)~\citep{gu2024mamba} have reshaped long-context sequence modeling, but
\emph{which forms of conditioning survive their training dynamics} is poorly
understood. We pick one concrete, widely-attempted conditioning method, FiLM~\citep{perez2018film}
regime conditioning of a Mamba backbone, and diagnose at small scale why it fails.
We then turn the failure into a reusable methods package.

\paragraph{The object of study.}
We attach a \emph{RegimeFiLM} modulator to a Mamba world-model backbone. The
modulator has two parts, a router that infers a soft regime latent, and a
hypernetwork that emits per-block channel-wise $(\gamma,\beta)$. Because the
selective-scan parameters $\Delta_t,\mathbf{B}_t,\mathbf{C}_t$ are input-dependent
linear projections of each block's input (\S\ref{sec:setup}), applying the affine
to the block \emph{input} is the natural, kernel-friendly way to push a regime
signal \emph{into} the dynamics.

\paragraph{The null}
The modulation decays to identity on every
objective and dataset (\S\ref{sec:null}). We find that an input-side affine's scale is a \emph{gauge} direction that folds
losslessly into the block's own projections, so the optimizer slides it back to
identity. Gauge absorption removes this constant scale on any axis. The collapse is specific to this drop-in conditioning on \emph{weakly-decodable} regimes.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A \textbf{decisive null} for input-affine FiLM conditioning of a
Mamba selective scan on the weakly-decodable regime axes we study, with an
exact zero-init control, on three datasets and a money
objective (\S\ref{sec:null}). (2) The \textbf{gauge-absorption mechanism} and its
\textbf{dataset-invariant} signature ($\tau\approx7000$, $R^2>0.999$), plus an
\textbf{output-side placement showing the null is overdetermined}
(App.~\ref{app:mech}). (3) A \textbf{small-scale, drop-in diagnostic protocol}
(\S\ref{sec:methods}), comprising the exact-control and escalation protocol, the toy
reference-scan in-kernel demo, the weight-decay ablation, and the char-Mamba LM
control bounding the null to weakly-decodable regimes.

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Limit order books, for a non-finance reader.}
A \emph{limit order book} (LOB) is the live ledger an exchange keeps for an asset.
It records how much volume rests on the buy and sell sides at each price near the
current mid-price. The model's task throughout this paper is to forecast the next short-horizon move
from the changing shape of this
ledger. We test the same conditioning method on three structurally different
books, so that one shared null across them speaks to the optimizer rather than to
a single dataset's quirks. \textbf{FI-2010} is regulated equities (Nasdaq Nordic), trading only during market hours on a
centralized exchange. \textbf{Kaggle crypto} spot books trade
24/7, giving heavier tails and thinner, faster-moving
books. \textbf{Polymarket} is a binary
prediction market, so its price is a probability in $[0,1]$ that a contract
settles to $0$ or $1$.

\paragraph{Selective State Space Models and Mamba3 Initialization.}
A selective SSM maps an input $x_t$ to an output $y_t$ via a latent state
$h_t=\bar{\mathbf{A}}h_{t-1}+\bar{\mathbf{B}}x_t$, $y_t=\mathbf{C}h_t$, where
$\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}},\mathbf{C}$ are the discretized state, input,
and output matrices. Mamba makes the scan parameters input-dependent:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection}
\Delta_t=\sigma(W_\Delta x_t),\quad \mathbf{B}_t=W_B x_t,\quad \mathbf{C}_t=W_C x_t,
\end{equation}
where $W_\Delta,W_B,W_C$ are learned projections, $\sigma$ the softplus nonlinearity, and $\Delta_t,\mathbf{B}_t,\mathbf{C}_t$ the now input-dependent scan parameters. As a result, \emph{any} affine transformation of $x_t$ propagates into all three scan
parameters. Mamba-3 further makes the state transition $A_t$ a data-dependent
projection of $x_t$, so the affine propagates there as well, which only adds to the
fold. The backbone is an $L{=}4$ stack of Mamba-3
SISO~\citep{lahoti2026mamba3} blocks. A router emits a soft regime distribution
$\bm{\alpha}_t=\softmax(W_r x_t^{(0)})\in\R^R$ from the stem output $x_t^{(0)}$,
where $R$ is the regime count ($R{=}4$ on FI-2010/Kaggle, $R{=}8$ on Polymarket).
A zero-initialized hypernetwork then maps this to bounded per-block FiLM
parameters $\gamma^{(\ell)}=1+\tanh(\tilde\gamma^{(\ell)})$,
$\beta^{(\ell)}=\tanh(\tilde\beta^{(\ell)})$ ($\ell$ the block index,
$\tilde\gamma,\tilde\beta$ the raw pre-$\tanh$ outputs), so
$\gamma{=}\mathbf{1},\beta{=}\mathbf{0}$ at step~0. The \textbf{input-side} site gates the block input,
$\gamma^{(\ell)}\odot\RMSNorm(x_t^{(\ell-1)})+\beta^{(\ell)}$, routing the regime
into $\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:selection}. A
\textbf{post-scan} control gates the block output instead, sharing the identical
modulator. A Switch-Transformer load-balance term~\citep{fedus2022switch},
disabled in the escalations, regularizes the marginal router entropy. Two
diagnostics gate every claim, $\mathrm{film}_g=\mathrm{mean}\,|\gamma-1|$ (zero iff
identity) and the router entropy $\mathrm{reg}_H$ (uniform maximum $\log R$).

\section{The Null, and Why It Holds}\label{sec:null}

\paragraph{Joint training drives FiLM inert.}
Across five objectives spanning two datasets (Table~\ref{tab:film_null}), $\mathrm{film}_g$ decays toward zero and $\mathrm{reg}_H$ sits at its
uniform maximum. Where a generalization gap reads
positive (direction $+0.008$, settlement $+0.18$), FiLM is provably inert, and the
treatment is \emph{absolutely worse} in both regimes. The gap is a distributional
artifact whose sign flips with head calibration, which is why the diagnostics are
load-bearing.

\paragraph{Further testing the null: Forced-active escalation (Polymarket).}
On
\textbf{Polymarket} ($R{=}8$) we force FiLM active ($\mathrm{film}_g=0.150$ at
step~0) and supervise the router
\emph{directly} on the spot efficiency-ratio axis, with the strongest settings (LR
multiplier~50, supervision weight~30, decoupled router/FiLM, fed observation
volatility). The modulation still monotonically decays ($\mathrm{film}_g$ from
$0.150$ to $0.043$, still falling), with $\mathrm{reg}_H=\log 8$ throughout. The
capacity is present but unused. On FI-2010, a router head trained alone on the
volatility bucket reaches $0.92$ agreement, so the capacity to encode that
axis exists, yet even there the modulation collapses (a linear probe on the frozen
representation is weaker, App.~\ref{app:mech}). On the \emph{predictability} axis,
which this escalation directly supervises, agreement is only weak
($\approx0.25$--$0.36$), so the collapse cannot be blamed on missing regime
information.

\paragraph{Why identity is a free direction.}
This instantiates a general \emph{absorbability criterion}. A channel-wise affine on
an activation immediately consumed by a learned linear map $A$ has its scale
absorbed into $A$, $A(\gamma\odot\hat x)=(A\diag(\gamma))\hat x$, with or without a
bias. Its shift is absorbed only when $A$ carries a trainable bias and no
nonlinearity intervenes. Modifying the in-kernel $W_\Delta,W_B,W_C$, by contrast,
is \emph{not} gauge-absorbable. Our input-side modulation
$\tilde{x}=\gamma\odot\hat{x}+\beta$ is consumed immediately by the selection
re-projections (Eq.~\eqref{eq:selection}), so its scale folds into
$W_{\{\Delta,B,C\}}$ with no change to the function the block computes. These
projections are bias-free up to a lone $dt$ bias that can absorb only the shift's
$\Delta$-component (App.~\ref{app:mech}). While the shift is not absorbed, it instead decays as an unrewarded direction under reconstruction
dominance (below). The scale is
therefore a \emph{gauge} direction, a flat, lossless valley
the optimizer slides along. This predicts a decay rate that is a property of the
parameterization, not the data. On the weakly-decodable LOB axes the supervised
router collapses to uniform, so $\gamma$ reduces to its constant part and weight
decay folds the remainder to identity (\S\ref{sec:methods}).

\paragraph{The strongest-null escalation yields a dataset-invariant $\tau$.}
We confirm our null with a \emph{cross-dataset strongest-null escalation}.
This time we raise the initialization further, to $\mathrm{film}_g=0.250$, and run
on two datasets with the load-balance term disabled. The modulation
monotonically decays on \emph{both} (from $0.250$ to $0.144$ on Kaggle, from
$0.250$ to $0.147$ on FI-2010), with $\mathrm{reg}_H\approx\log 4$ throughout. An exponential fit
$a\,e^{-t/\tau}+c$ matches every run at $R^2>0.99$ (seeds 0--2, $n{=}6$; toy below
adds seeds 0--3), with a dataset-invariant $\tau=6668\pm478$ steps (FI-2010
$6964\pm922$, Kaggle $6373\pm454$; $a\approx0.30$, $c\le0$). The decay is monotone to the last logged step, with the
endpoint still falling at $\approx0.15$ and identity as the extrapolated limit.
The fitted asymptote also lies at or below identity ($c\lesssim-0.05$, only more
negative when the lower bound on $c$ is relaxed), so the trajectory extrapolates
toward identity rather than to a positive plateau. That the rate is essentially
\emph{identical} across two very different markets points to the optimizer, not
the data (Figure~\ref{fig:escalation_decay}). Repeating this escalation on the
\emph{best-decoded} volatility bucket instead (which a frozen-router head fits to
$0.92$ on FI-2010) gives the same decay to $0.144$ on both datasets ($\tau\approx6700$/$6610$,
$R^2=0.9999$, $\mathrm{reg}_H\approx\log 4$), so the collapse is gauge absorption,
not an undecodable supervised axis.


\paragraph{The null is overdetermined.}
Under
the identical escalation, the output-side gate does \emph{not} escape. Each block
ends in a \emph{bias-free} output projection, so the output scale folds into it,
$\gamma\odot(W_{\text{out}}h)=(\diag(\gamma)W_{\text{out}})h$ (from $0.250$ to
$0.146$ on FI-2010, from $0.250$ to $0.143$ on Kaggle, same $\tau$, $R^2>0.999$,
seed~0 as the input-side run). The one non-absorbable component, the additive shift, decays in
lockstep under reconstruction dominance (weight decay acting on a direction the
reconstruction loss does not reward). Two independent routes remove any surviving direction, and both share a single
driver, the gauge absorption mechanism we name. A modulation that escapes
both would have to act \emph{inside} the fused kernel, and we demonstrate a specific parametrization at toy-scale that persists (derivations and the output-side fit in App.~\ref{app:mech}).

\section{Small-Scale Methods That Establish the  of the Null}\label{sec:methods}

Three elementary probes (full numbers in App.~\ref{app:methods})
prove the asserted boundary. \textbf{(i) Folding (toy
reference scan).} An input-affine modulation folds into primed host projections to
floating-point precision and decays to identity. The \emph{same} modulation
applied to the projected $\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ \emph{inside} the scan is
non-absorbable instead ($\sim10^4\times$ larger fold residual) and
\textbf{persists} (seeds 0--3). It survives the very training that decays the
affine form. \textbf{(ii) Gauge vs.\ regularization.} With weight decay off, the
input-affine is no longer pulled to identity, and its decay time constant blows up
${\sim}46\times$. The decay is \textbf{weight-decay-driven over a flat
loss}, not earned. \textbf{(iii) A language-model control bounds the null.} A
2-layer char-Mamba LM on prose vs.\ Python, with the domain-supervised regime and
the same input-affine FiLM, does \emph{not} collapse. With a strongly-decodable
regime (router accuracy $\ge0.9$; $0.91$ at the last logged step), $\mathrm{film}_g$
rises from $0.11$ to $0.52$ instead. Gauge absorption always removes the \emph{constant} component. The input-dependent component survives only when the regime is decodable enough to be
worth encoding. \textbf{While decodability is necessary, it is not sufficient}. The
modulation must also lower the training loss. Even the best-decoded LOB axis
(FI-2010 volatility, $0.92$ agreement) decays, since it is redundant with what the
backbone already computes from the input. Code-vs-prose is not similarly redundant,
since it changes next-character statistics. (Single-seed toy control; the qualitative link
between a decodable router and persistence is clear, but the plateau value is not
load-bearing.)

\section{The Substrate Is Not the Subject}\label{sec:substrate}

The financial datasets are a demonstration substrate. The backbone is a Mamba-3
world model in the DreamerV3/Drama lineage~\citep{hafner2023dreamerv3, wang2024drama},
but none of this is the claim. Two checks confirm the backbone carries no weight.
\textbf{(i) No backbone dominates.} A matched-parameter ablation (Mamba-1/2/3 SISO
and a Mamba-3-matched pre-norm Transformer) shows no backbone leads on both
FI-2010 and Kaggle. The single-seed cross-dataset gaps are within run-to-run noise
(Table~\ref{tab:backbone}, App.~\ref{app:tables}). \textbf{(ii) The surviving economic edge is architecture-independent.} On the
identical $645$-market held-out BTC set, a selective-participation gate turns a
directional settlement probability into a survivable high-predictability edge (95\%
CI $[+\$2{,}445,+\$7{,}747]$ over the $127$ traded markets). A logistic regression
on the same spot-conditioned features \emph{reproduces and exceeds} it on that same
set ($+\$6{,}914$ vs.\ the settlement head's $+\$5{,}147$). The operative
variable is therefore the features and the gate, not the sequence model
(App.~\ref{app:coda}).

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Scale redundancy and scale-invariance.}
That a channel-wise affine before a linear map is redundant with that map is a
known theme in the normalization literature. \citet{vanlaarhoven2017l2},
\citet{hoffer2018norm}, and \citet{li2020exp} show that weights feeding a
normalization layer are scale-invariant, which recasts $L_2$/weight decay as an
effective-learning-rate schedule rather than a capacity control. Our null extends
this redundancy to a setting that has not been analyzed before. Here the affine is
a per-regime, \emph{input-dependent} FiLM that folds into the selective scan's own
$W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$. We add an optimizer-implicating, dataset-invariant decay
signature ($\tau\approx7000$, $R^2>0.999$), an output-side placement that makes
the null \emph{overdetermined}, and the fact that the only kernel-friendly
drop-in form is exactly the absorbable one. Mamba-2 recasts the scan as structured
masked attention~\citep{dao2024mamba2}. DeepLOB~\citep{zhang2019deeplob} attains
$0.628$ macro-F1 on FI-2010 horizon-10 labels, versus a $0.261$ majority-class
floor, confirming our data carries signal.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The whole apparatus was run on a single consumer GPU
(${\approx}2$--$4\times10^{16}$ FLOPs total, App.~\ref{app:methods}). The full
study is ${\sim}25$ runs at ${\sim}14$~minutes each, about six GPU-hours
end-to-end.
The constructive controls that \emph{bound}
the null, the weight-decay-off ablation, the LM control, and the output-side fit,
are single-seed (the in-kernel toy is seeds 0--3). The null itself, by contrast,
is multi-setting and multi-seed (full limitations, App.~\ref{app:methods}).
\textbf{Takeaway.} Across the settings studied, affine conditioning applied
outside the selective scan is consistently removed by optimization, while
modulation acting directly on the scan parameters can persist. Empirically, we
measure decay only for Mamba-3, leaving broader applicability an open question.  Likewise, while the in-kernel toy shows persistence at reference-scan
scale, whether such modulation improves a real task is unknown. The practical
outcome is a \textbf{drop-in diagnostic protocol} (boxed in
App.~\ref{app:methods}): a roughly 14-minute single-GPU test that can distinguish
potentially persistent conditioning mechanisms from those a matched scan may
silently absorb.

\section{Mechanism: the output-side placement in full}\label{app:mech}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.50\linewidth]{figures/film_g_escalation_decay.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Strongest-null test, and its placement-invariance.} Modulation is forced active at step 0 ($\mathrm{film}_g=0.250$ ) while router entropy remains fixed at  $\log 4$ (bottom). On both datasets, $\mathrm{film}_g$ decays monotonically toward identity (top). Input-side (solid) and post-scan (dotted) gates follow nearly identical trajectories $R^2>0.99$ (seeds 0--2, $n{=}6$). The fitted decay constant is $\tau=6668\pm478$. (App.~\ref{app:mech}, App.~B).}
\label{fig:escalation_decay}
\end{figure}
The output-side gate applies $y'=\gamma\odot y+\beta$ to the block output. Each
Mamba-3 block ends in a \emph{bias-free} linear output projection
$y=W_{\text{out}}h$, so a channel-wise output scale folds exactly into it,
$\gamma\odot(W_{\text{out}}h)=(\diag(\gamma)W_{\text{out}})h$, the same gauge move
as the input side but on the block's other face. The additive shift $\beta$ is
genuinely non-absorbable. There is no output bias, and a nonlinear $\RMSNorm$
separates it from the next block's projections. Yet under forced activation and ER
supervision, $\mathrm{film}_g$ and its additive counterpart
$\mathrm{film}_b=\mathrm{mean}\,|\beta|$ monotonically decay in lockstep on
both datasets ($\mathrm{film}_g$ from $0.250$ to $0.146$ on FI-2010, from $0.250$
to $0.143$ on Kaggle; $\mathrm{film}_b$ tracks within $0.005$), with
$\mathrm{reg}_H\approx\log 4$ throughout. The fit gives the same dataset-invariant signature ($a\approx0.30$,
$\tau\approx7065$ FI-2010 / $6598$ Kaggle, asymptote $c\lesssim-0.05$ and below
identity with the bound relaxed, $R^2>0.999$).
Two probes localize the failure. The jointly-supervised router is uniform per
step ($\approx\log 4$), with near-chance ER agreement ($0.25$--$0.31$). A frozen
linear probe on the unmodulated features decodes the predictability bucket only
weakly ($0.33$--$0.36$ vs.\ $0.25$) and the volatility bucket somewhat better
($0.43$--$0.47$); this measures linear decodability of the representation, distinct
from the $0.92$ a supervised router head reaches on the volatility bucket on
FI-2010 (\S\ref{sec:null}). Yet the volatility-axis gap is also null.

\paragraph{Input-side shift decomposition.}
The input-side shift decomposes along the block's four consumption paths. The
$\Delta$-path ends in a lone trainable $dt$ bias inside the softplus (suppressed
in the simplified Eq.~\eqref{eq:selection}), so the $\Delta$-component of a
constant shift folds into it exactly; this is the only host parameter that can
absorb any part of the shift. Measured directly, all four blocks' $dt$-bias norms
drift smoothly and near-identically by ${\sim}26\%$ over the 3{,}000-step
escalation, so this fold is visible in training, not only in the algebra. The
$\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ components are blocked
by per-state norms interposed between the projections and the kernel (a post-norm
bias cannot absorb a pre-norm offset), and the gate and scan-input paths carry no
bias at all. The shift as a whole therefore cannot fold into the host, and it
decays as an unrewarded direction under reconstruction dominance, exactly as
observed.


\section{Methods detail}\label{app:methods}

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\textbf{Drop-in diagnostic protocol.}
(1)~Force the gate active (e.g.\ $\mathrm{film}_g\approx0.15$--$0.25$ at step~0)
and supervise the router directly on the candidate regime axis.
(2)~Train ${\sim}3{,}000$ steps (${\sim}14$~min on one consumer GPU).
(3)~Fit $a\,e^{-t/\tau}+c$ to the $\mathrm{film}_g$ trajectory. Decay toward
identity with $c\lesssim0$ means the placement is gauge-absorbable and a matched
scan will silently undo it; a rise to a positive plateau means the conditioning
is being used.}}
\medskip

All toy experiments use the pure-PyTorch reference scan that \texttt{mamba\_ssm}
ships (the slow path the fused-kernel hardware limit does not touch), in seconds on
the same single 4080. \textbf{Folding.} The recovered projections satisfy
$W_\bullet^{\text{fit}}\approx W_\bullet^{\text{base}}\diag(\gamma)$ (for
$\bullet\in\{\Delta,B,C\}$) to round-off
(input-affine residual $1.2\times10^{-6}$). The in-kernel placement, by contrast,
leaves a residual $1.3\times10^{-2}$, because a per-channel scale on the
post-softplus $\Delta$ cannot be matched by any linear reparameterization of the
pre-softplus projection. \textbf{Dynamics.} Input-affine decays from
$0.086$ to $0.010$ with weight decay on; in-kernel persists, rising from
$0.093$ to $0.175$ (seeds 0--3). \textbf{Weight-decay ablation.} With weight
decay off, the input-affine stays near its forced-active init (toy, from
$0.086$ to $0.342$; full-model $\tau$ goes from $\approx6685$, $R^2{=}0.9999$, to
$\approx3.1\times10^5$, $R^2{=}0.48$, a ${\sim}46\times$ blow-up). \textbf{LM control.} A 2-layer char-level
Mamba LM, built on \texttt{mamba\_ssm} blocks, trained on prose vs.\ Python with a
context-aware domain-supervised router and the same input-affine FiLM. The router
reaches $\ge0.9$ accuracy ($0.98$ at peak, $0.91$ at the last logged step), and
$\mathrm{film}_g$ rises from $0.11$ to $0.52$. We
run Mamba-3 SISO throughout because the MIMO kernel's dynamic shared-memory
footprint exceeds the 4080 cap at every warp-valid (rank, chunk) configuration.
This is the same kernel region a surviving modulation would have to occupy.

\paragraph{Estimated compute (FLOPs).}
Each pretraining run is ${\sim}3000$ optimizer steps at a $64{\times}64$-token
batch on FI-2010/Kaggle ($128{\times}64$ on Polymarket), giving
$T\approx1.2$--$2.5\times10^7$ tokens per run. With $N\approx10.3$M backbone
parameters, the dominant cost is $6NT\approx0.8$--$1.5\times10^{15}$ FLOPs per
run (${\sim}14$~min on the 4080). The full study, the null table, the
cross-dataset sweep, the input- and output-side escalations, the weight-decay
ablation, and the five-backbone matched ablation (${\sim}25$ runs total), costs
${\approx}2$--$4\times10^{16}$ FLOPs. The toy reference-scan and char-LM
controls are negligible by comparison. Every experiment is thus
$\gtrsim10^{3}\times$ below the $10^{20}$ frontier-scale reference, on one
consumer GPU.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The \emph{null} is multi-setting and multi-seed; the \emph{constructive} claims
that bound it are smaller. The full-model
weight-decay-off ablation and the LM control are single-seed, though the in-kernel
toy adds seeds 0--3. The in-kernel ``survives'' result is at toy reference-scan
scale, since the MIMO placement is outside the 4080 envelope. The substrate is
finance-only, so the architecture-universality of the gauge algebra is argued, not
demonstrated at language-model scale. None of these bears on the null itself.

\section{Deflated economic coda}\label{app:coda}

Given the spot path that defines Polymarket settlement, the model produces an
over-confident but directional settlement probability. We trade it with a
selective-participation strategy that bets only when a train-frozen efficiency-ratio
gate fires. All totals below are on the \emph{identical} $645$-market held-out BTC
set, against a mandatory no-model naive control that loses $-\$1{,}008$. The counts
$127$ and $243$ are the per-arm traded/evaluable subsets a market-block bootstrap
resamples, not different evaluation sets.

On that set the gated settlement head totals $+\$5{,}147$, a 3-seed expected-value
head averages $+\$4{,}779$, and a logistic regression on the same spot-conditioned
features \emph{reproduces and exceeds} both at $+\$6{,}914$ (Brier score $0.146$,
the mean-squared error between forecast probability and outcome, lower is better).
Each is a survivable, naive-beating edge. The settlement head's high-predictability
block has $\mathrm{P(profit)}\approx1.0$ with 95\% CI $[+\$2{,}445,+\$7{,}747]$ over
its $127$ traded markets, and the logistic reaches $\mathrm{P(profit)}=0.9994$ with
95\% CI $[+\$2{,}844,+\$10{,}880]$ over its $243$. The operative variable is
therefore the features and the gate, not the sequence model. The settlement head's
low-predictability block is marginal ($+\$51$, $\mathrm{P}=0.63$); a direct
expected-value head makes it robust instead ($\mathrm{P}=0.98$--$0.99$), collapsing
the high-vs-low PnL spread $6.2\times$ at comparable total. Only the predictability
gate is confirmatory; the depth-band and early-market cuts are exploratory, and the
reliability figures are recorded-vintage structural diagnostics.
