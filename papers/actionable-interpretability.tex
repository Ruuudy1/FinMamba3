\begin{abstract}
Selective state-space models such as Mamba have reshaped long-context sequence
modeling. Yet which forms of conditioning \emph{survive} their training dynamics is
poorly understood. We study FiLM-style regime conditioning of a Mamba backbone: a
per-block, channel-wise affine on each block's input. This is the natural drop-in
way to steer the input-dependent selective-scan parameters
$\Delta,\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}$. We find a \textbf{decisive null} with an actionable
mechanistic explanation. Under joint training on weakly decodable market regimes,
the modulation collapses near identity
($\mathrm{film}_g\approx0.004$--$0.010$) across FI-2010 equities, Kaggle crypto spot
books, and Polymarket prediction markets, and it cannot be forced active even by
direct supervision. The scale component of an input-side affine is a
\textbf{gauge} direction: it folds losslessly into the block's own
$\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ projections. In the Mamba3 blocks used
here, the additive shift has no matching bias that can absorb it in full (only its
$\Delta$-component can fold, into the $dt$ bias) and decays as an unrewarded
component.
Forced-active FI-2010/Kaggle runs decay monotonically with descriptive
order-of-thousands exponential fits ($R^2>0.999$). An output-side placement shows
the same split: scale is absorbed into the bias-free output projection, while the
shift is unrewarded and also decays. The usable consequence is negative and
diagnostic. Do not expect input- or output-affine regime conditioning of a
selective scan to persist unless the regime is strongly decodable. Before crediting
any conditioning, verify the gate is active. An inert gate reported beside a
positive generalization gap means the gap is an \emph{artifact}, not a conditioning
effect. The positive design rule is scoped: a surviving modulation should act on
$\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ \emph{inside} the fused kernel, a target validated at
toy reference-scan scale (seeds 0--3) but not yet demonstrated at full world-model
or foundation-model scale.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Conditioning a network on side information (a task ID, a context vector, an
inferred regime) is among the most common things a practitioner does, and FiLM
(a channel-wise affine $\gamma\odot x+\beta$) \citep{perez2018film} is the
default mechanism. When the backbone is a \textbf{selective state-space model}
(Mamba \citep{gu2024mamba}), there is an especially tempting place to inject it.
Mamba's scan parameters $\Delta_t,\mathbf{B}_t,\mathbf{C}_t$ are
\emph{input-dependent} linear projections of each block's input. An affine on that
input therefore flows straight into the dynamics, and, crucially, it needs
\textbf{no change to the fused CUDA/TileLang kernel}. This is the drop-in form
everyone reaches for first. We show it does not work, explain mechanistically why,
and turn that explanation into two things a practitioner can use: a diagnostic that
catches a specific false-positive, and a design rule that says where a surviving
modulation has to live.

\paragraph{The finding.}
We introduce a \emph{RegimeFiLM} modulator that infers a categorical latent
regime and emits per-block affine parameters $(\gamma,\beta)$. It is
zero-initialized, so at step 0 the modulated backbone is identical to the
unmodulated baseline (an exact paired control). We then apply the strongest
activation pressure we could: forced-active initialization plus direct supervision
of the router. The modulation still decays to identity on every market objective
and dataset we test. The cause is a \textbf{gauge symmetry} for the multiplicative
component. A constant channel-wise scale on the block input folds losslessly into
the host projections $W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$ as a right-multiplied diagonal
$W_\bullet\diag(\gamma)$. Mamba3's input projections are bias-free up to a lone
$dt$ bias that can absorb only the shift's $\Delta$-component, so the additive
shift is not gauge in our implementation; it has no rewarded role in these weakly
decodable regimes and decays as an unrewarded component. Joint training therefore
returns $(\gamma,\beta)\to(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0})$ by a scale-gauge plus
unrewarded-shift mechanism. Forced-active FI-2010 and Kaggle runs decay
monotonically, with order-of-thousands descriptive time constants ($R^2>0.999$)
that are similar across datasets because the scale-absorption direction is a
property of the \emph{block parameterization}, not the data. Moving the gate to the
block \emph{output} does not escape this: the scale folds into the bias-free output
projection, and the residual shift again earns nothing.

\paragraph{Why this is actionable.}
Two upshots change what a practitioner does.
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{A diagnostic that catches a false-positive.} The activation
  diagnostics $\mathrm{film}_g=\mathrm{mean}\,|\gamma-1|$ and the router entropy
  $\mathrm{reg}_H$ (uniform maximum $\log R$) are \textbf{load-bearing}. In our
  data a generalization-gap metric reads \emph{positive}, with the ``right'' sign,
  while FiLM is provably inert and the treatment is \emph{absolutely worse} in both
  regimes. The gap is a distributional ($\mathrm{high}-\mathrm{low}$) artifact
  whose sign even flips with head calibration. A practitioner who reports
  ``conditioning improved the out-of-regime gap'' without checking that the gate is
  actually \emph{on} can ship an artifact.
\item \emph{A design rule that says where to put the gate.} If you want regime
  conditioning of a selective scan to \emph{survive} training, the immediately
  actionable negative rule is to skip input- and output-affine placements when the
  router is uniform. The constructive placement must act \textbf{between the
  block's bounding projections}, modulating $\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ inside
  the fused kernel. We validate that target at toy reference-scan scale; we do not
  claim a full world-model or foundation-model in-kernel implementation here.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Demonstration substrate.}
To stress the mechanism on genuinely different data we build a Mamba-3 world model
for limit-order-book (LOB) microstructure in the DreamerV3/Drama lineage
\citep{hafner2023dreamerv3, wang2024drama, lahoti2026mamba3}, exercised on FI-2010
equities, Kaggle crypto spot books, and Polymarket prediction markets
(\S\ref{sec:substrate}). The backbone is a substrate, not a claim. Under matched
parameters, \emph{no} backbone dominates across datasets (\S\ref{sec:ablation}),
exactly what a result about how selective scans \emph{train}, rather than which one
is best, should predict. The same study yields a deflated economic coda
(\S\ref{sec:coda}) that a logistic regression on the same features reproduces, so it
is not attributable to the architecture.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Selective state-space models and Mamba.}
Structured state-space models (S4, S5, Mamba) give $O(n)$-complexity alternatives
to Transformers \citep{gu2022efficiently, smith2023s5, gu2024mamba}. Mamba
introduced \emph{selective} state spaces in which $\Delta$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are
input-dependent, enabling content-aware filtering; Mamba-2 \citep{dao2024mamba2}
formalized the duality with structured masked attention, and Mamba-3
\citep{lahoti2026mamba3} adds multi-input/multi-output (MIMO) transitions. The
property we exploit is that $\Delta_t,\mathbf{B}_t,\mathbf{C}_t$ are learned linear
projections of the block input, and this is exactly what makes the scale component
of an input-affine a gauge direction. We are not aware of prior work characterizing the training-time
fate of FiLM-style conditioning of a selective scan.

\paragraph{FiLM and feature-wise conditioning.}
FiLM \citep{perez2018film} injects context as a channel-wise affine and is the
default conditioning primitive across vision, multi-task, and regime-embedding
settings \citep{lim2021time}. Our result is about \emph{where} in a selective-scan
block an affine can be placed and still survive joint training. This is a gauge
constraint specific to the input-dependent projection structure, orthogonal to
FiLM's expressive-power story.

\paragraph{Interpretability of training dynamics and weight symmetries.}
A line of interpretability work studies \emph{how} solutions form rather than
\emph{what} a fixed network computes, including reparameterization and gauge
symmetries that leave the function unchanged while reshaping the loss landscape
\citep{dinh2017sharp}. In the normalization literature, a channel-wise affine before a linear map is known to be redundant with that map.
\citet{vanlaarhoven2017l2} shows weights feeding a normalization layer are
scale-invariant, so $L_2$ regularization acts on their \emph{scale}, and thus on
the effective learning rate, rather than as capacity control;
\citet{hoffer2018norm} and \citet{li2020exp} re-derive weight decay as an
effective-learning-rate schedule on the same symmetry. Our null is the same redundancy in a new setting. The absorbed component is
not a static normalization gain but the scale of a per-regime, \emph{input-dependent}
FiLM that folds into the selective scan's own $W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$ projections. What is new is
(i) the input-dependent selective-scan instantiation; (ii) the observed monotone
forced-active decay, with similar order-of-thousands descriptive fits
($R^2>0.999$; architecture-general by the algebra of the fold, not yet shown at FM
scale), attributing the collapse to the optimizer; (iii) the output-side placement
showing the same scale-gauge plus unrewarded-shift split;
and (iv) the practical consequence that the only kernel-friendly, drop-in form is
exactly the absorbable one. The redundancy is therefore the default a practitioner
reaches for, not a corner case. The actionable contribution is to turn that
symmetry into a measurable diagnostic ($\mathrm{film}_g$, $\mathrm{reg}_H$) and a
placement rule.

\paragraph{LOB models and regime-switching (substrate).}
DeepLOB \citep{zhang2019deeplob} and successors are discriminative LOB models on
FI-2010 \citep{ntakaris2018benchmark}; generative LOB work
\citep{nagy2023lobs5, backhouse2025painting} targets simulation fidelity;
classical regime-switching and Bayesian changepoint detection identify discrete
states from price processes \citep{hamilton1989new, adams2007bayesian}. We use
these only as the demonstration substrate and benchmark context, not as baselines
for the conditioning result.

\section{Setup: FiLM Conditioning of a Selective Scan}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Selective SSM.}
A discretized state-space layer maps an input $x_t\in\R^{d}$ to an output $y_t$ via a latent state
$h_t$: $h_t=\bar{\mathbf{A}}h_{t-1}+\bar{\mathbf{B}}x_t$, $y_t=\mathbf{C}h_t$, where
$\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}}$ are the discretized state-transition and input matrices and $\mathbf{C}$ the output matrix.
Mamba makes the parameters \textbf{input-dependent}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection}
\Delta_t=\sigma(W_\Delta\,x_t),\qquad \mathbf{B}_t=W_B\,x_t,\qquad
\mathbf{C}_t=W_C\,x_t,
\end{equation}
where $W_\Delta,W_B,W_C$ are learned projection matrices, $\sigma$ is the softplus nonlinearity, $\Delta_t$ is the per-step discretization size, and $\mathbf{B}_t,\mathbf{C}_t$ are the now input-dependent scan parameters, so \emph{any affine transformation of $x_t$ propagates directly into all three
scan parameters}. Mamba-3 further makes the state transition $A_t$ a data-dependent projection of $x_t$, so the affine propagates there too. This is the selection mechanism we attempt to steer.

\paragraph{RegimeFiLM modulator.}
A regime head produces a soft categorical
$\bm{\alpha}_t=\softmax(W_r\,x_t^{(0)})\in\R^R$ over $R$ regimes
($R=4$ for FI-2010/Kaggle and $R=8$ for Polymarket), with router weights $W_r$
acting on the stem output $x_t^{(0)}$. It reads out a regime embedding
$r_t=\bm{\alpha}_t^\top E$, where $E$ is a learned regime-embedding table; a hypernetwork maps
$r_t$ to per-block affine parameters, bounded for bf16 stability:
\begin{equation}
\gamma^{(\ell)}=1+\tanh(\tilde{\gamma}^{(\ell)})\in(0,2),\qquad
\beta^{(\ell)}=\tanh(\tilde{\beta}^{(\ell)})\in(-1,1),
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ indexes the block, $\gamma^{(\ell)},\beta^{(\ell)}$ are that block's FiLM scale and shift, and $\tilde{\gamma}^{(\ell)},\tilde{\beta}^{(\ell)}$ are the hypernetwork's raw (pre-$\tanh$) outputs.
The hypernetwork is \textbf{zero-initialized}, so at step 0
$\gamma^{(\ell)}=\mathbf{1},\beta^{(\ell)}=\mathbf{0}$ for all $\ell$ and the
modulated backbone is identical to the unmodulated baseline, a clean and exact
paired control. A Switch-Transformer load-balancing term \citep{fedus2022switch}
maximizes the entropy of the \emph{batch-averaged} regime distribution,
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{regime}}=\lambda_{\text{ent}}(\log R-H[\bar{\bm{\alpha}}])$ (with $\lambda_{\text{ent}}$ a weight, $H[\cdot]$ the entropy, and $\bar{\bm{\alpha}}$ the batch-averaged router distribution),
regularizing marginal usage while leaving per-step assignments free to be peaky.

\paragraph{Two application sites.}
The \textbf{input-affine} site routes the regime into the scan through the
selection mechanism:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:input}
x_t^{(\ell)}=x_t^{(\ell-1)}+\mathrm{Mamba3}\!\big(\gamma^{(\ell)}\odot\RMSNorm(x_t^{(\ell-1)})+\beta^{(\ell)}\big),
\end{equation}
where $x_t^{(\ell)}$ is block $\ell$'s output (input $x_t^{(\ell-1)}$, the previous block's output), $\odot$ the element-wise product, $\RMSNorm$ RMS normalization, and $\mathrm{Mamba3}(\cdot)$ the selective-scan block of Eq.~\eqref{eq:selection}.
As a control we also expose a \textbf{post-scan} site that gates the block
\emph{output}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:postscan}
x_t^{(\ell)}=x_t^{(\ell-1)}+\gamma^{(\ell)}\odot\mathrm{Mamba3}\!\big(\RMSNorm(x_t^{(\ell-1)})\big)+\beta^{(\ell)}.
\end{equation}
Both share the identical modulator; only the site differs. This isolates the gauge
structure (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}): the input-side scale folds into
$W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$, the post-scan scale folds into the bias-free output projection
$W_{\text{out}}$, and the additive shift cannot be absorbed in full in either
placement.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.}
Activation metrics gate every claim:
$\mathrm{film}_g=\mathrm{mean}\,|\gamma-1|$ (deviation of the multiplicative gate
from identity), its additive counterpart $\mathrm{film}_b=\mathrm{mean}\,|\beta|$
(reported for the output-side shift in \S\ref{sec:gauge}), and the router entropy
$\mathrm{reg}_H$ (uniform maximum $\log R$, with $R$ the regime count; $\log 4$ on
FI-2010/Kaggle, $\log 8$ on Polymarket). \textbf{A positive generalization gap with an
inert FiLM is an artifact, not a regime-conditioning effect}. This is the central
methodological point.

\section{Demonstration Substrate (condensed)}\label{sec:substrate}

\paragraph{Limit order books, for a non-finance reader.}
A \emph{limit order book} (LOB) is the live ledger an exchange keeps for an asset.
At each instant it records how much volume rests on the buy and sell sides at each
price near the mid-price, and it updates many times a second as orders arrive,
cancel, and execute. The task is to forecast the next short-horizon move (price
direction, volatility, or order flow) from the changing shape of this ladder. We
span three \emph{structurally different} kinds of book on purpose, so that one
shared null across them speaks to the optimizer rather than to a single dataset.
\textbf{FI-2010} is regulated equities (Nasdaq Nordic): centralized, fixed-hours,
and discrete-tick, with mature and comparatively stable microstructure.
\textbf{Crypto} spot books trade 24/7 with no circuit breakers across many venues,
giving heavier tails and thinner, faster-moving books; we sample them at
$1\,$s/$1\,$min/$5\,$min resolutions. \textbf{Polymarket} is a binary prediction
market rather than a continuous price. Each contract settles to $0$ or $1$ at a
known expiry, so its price is a probability in $[0,1]$ set by a time-to-expiry
clock and the reference move since open. Equity microstructure, crypto volatility,
and a bounded settling probability are deliberately far apart.

To exercise the mechanism on genuinely different distributions we instantiate the
modulated backbone inside a Mamba-3 world model for LOB microstructure
(DreamerV3/Drama lineage \citep{hafner2023dreamerv3, wang2024drama}): a
Transformer-over-depth encoder feeds a $16\times16$ categorical posterior and an
$L{=}4$ stack of Mamba-3 SISO blocks ($d_{\text{state}}{=}128$), trained with the
standard DreamerV3 loss (reconstruction $+$ dynamics/representation KL) plus light
auxiliary heads. The rest of the machinery (episodic memory, a Hawkes head, the
imagination loop, the EV-head internals) is confined to an anonymized supplementary
implementation; no conditioning result depends on any of it.

\paragraph{Regime axis.}
Beyond realized volatility we use a \textbf{predictability} axis on the underlying
spot series (the asset's live market price). Its primary measure is the Kaufman
efficiency ratio
$\mathrm{ER}=|s_n-s_0|/\sum_t|s_t-s_{t-1}|$, where $s_t$ is the spot price at step
$t$ within the window and $s_0,s_n$ are its first and last values; $\mathrm{ER}$ is
near 1 for a clean trend and near 0 for a random wander. We also use permutation
entropy and the Hurst exponent. These measures serve two roles. They split the
held-out evaluation into forecastable versus random-walk regimes, and, when the
router collapses, they let us \emph{supervise it directly}. ``Is this window
forecastable'' is the most defensible regime signal we have. A router that will not
encode even this axis is the strongest form of the null.

\paragraph{Datasets.}
(i) Polymarket binary-outcome crypto LOBs (BTC/ETH/SOL up/down at 5/15/60-min
intervals, with the underlying Chainlink/Binance spot path); (ii) the public
FI-2010 benchmark \citep{ntakaris2018benchmark}; (iii) Kaggle high-frequency crypto
spot books (BTC/ETH/ADA at 1\,s / 1\,min / 5\,min). Held-out markets are split by
predictability (primary) and realized volatility (secondary), both reported.
\textbf{All thresholds are fit on the training split and frozen before any held-out
evaluation}, and baseline and treatment share the seed (an exact paired control).

\section{The Null and Its Mechanism}\label{sec:mechanism}

\subsection{RegimeFiLM is a decisive null}\label{sec:null}

Table~\ref{tab:film_null} reports the central diagnostic across five objectives
spanning two datasets and a money objective. In every family FiLM collapses to
identity under joint training: $\mathrm{film}_g$ decays toward zero and
$\mathrm{reg}_H$ sits at its uniform maximum ($\log R$). Where a generalization gap
reads positive (direction $+0.008$, settlement $+0.18$, by sign), FiLM is provably
inert, the treatment is \emph{absolutely worse} in both regimes, and the gap is a
$\mathrm{high}-\mathrm{low}$ distributional artifact whose sign even flips with head
calibration, which is exactly why the activation diagnostics are load-bearing. Read
\emph{without} the gate diagnostic, both gaps carry the sign a successful
conditioner would produce, and we would have reported a spurious win. Only the inert
$\mathrm{film}_g$ exposes the gap as an artifact, and this is the reading we
ourselves nearly published.

\paragraph{Polymarket forced-active supervision test.}
Forcing FiLM active on Polymarket (zero-init scale raised so
$\mathrm{film}_g=0.150$ at step 0) and supervising the router \emph{directly} on the
spot efficiency-ratio axis with
the strongest settings (LR multiplier 50, supervision weight 30, decoupled
router/FiLM, fed observation volatility) still decays the modulation monotonically
toward identity ($\mathrm{film}_g$:
$0.150\to0.119\to0.100\to0.074\to0.056\to0.043$, still falling), with
$\mathrm{reg}_H=\log 8$ throughout. The capacity is present but unused. On FI-2010,
a router head trained alone on the \emph{volatility} bucket reaches $0.92$ agreement,
a capacity check (a linear probe on the frozen representation is weaker,
App.~\ref{app:probe}), yet even there the modulation collapses.
On the \emph{predictability} axis, where this escalation supervises the router, the
regime decodes only weakly ($\approx0.25$--$0.36$).

\subsection{The mechanism: scale gauge plus unrewarded shift}\label{sec:gauge}

\paragraph{Cross-dataset collapse points at the optimizer.}
We take the architecture finding to FI-2010 (re-examined under the
predictability-primary axis) and the Kaggle crypto spot book (BTC/ETH/ADA at three
resolutions), judged by forecasting metrics alone. The zero-initialized modulation
never leaves identity: $\mathrm{film}_g\approx0.004$ and $\mathrm{reg}_H=\log 4$ in
every setting (Table~\ref{tab:film_null_xdata}). With FiLM an exact paired control,
every held-out gap is statistically indistinguishable from zero (all 95\% CIs
include 0) on both regime axes. The split is non-degenerate. High-efficiency-ratio
reference windows carry genuinely more structure on all three predictability
measures, and DeepLOB attains $0.628$ macro-F1 on the FI-2010 horizon-10 labels. So
the null is a negative on \emph{forecastable} data, not a no-signal artifact.

\paragraph{The collapse is optimizer-driven.}
The predictability-supervised strongest-null test (forced-active FiLM, router
supervised directly on the efficiency-ratio bucket), applied at the
\textbf{input-side} gate, decays $\mathrm{film}_g$ monotonically at every logged
step on \emph{both} datasets ($0.250\to0.144$ Kaggle, $0.250\to0.147$ FI-2010),
with $\mathrm{reg}_H\approx\log 4$ throughout \emph{even though the load-balance
term is disabled}. The uniform router is therefore not a load-balance artifact. A
descriptive exponential fit $a\,e^{-t/\tau}+c$ matches both trajectories at
$R^2>0.999$ with similar order-of-thousands parameters ($a\approx0.30$,
$\tau\approx6.7$--$7.2$k steps). The decay is monotone, with a negative slope
sustained to the last logged step (endpoint $\mathrm{film}_g\approx0.15$, still
falling), and the fitted asymptote lies at or below identity ($c\lesssim-0.05$,
only more negative when the lower bound on $c$ is relaxed). We therefore rest the
claim on the observed monotone decay and treat identity as the extrapolated limit,
not as a precisely identified invariant. That the decay scale is similar across two
very different markets points at the optimizer, not the data
(Figure~\ref{fig:escalation_decay}). Repeating this escalation on the
\emph{best-decoded} volatility bucket instead (the axis a frozen-router head fits to
$0.92$ on FI-2010) gives the same decay to $0.144$ on both datasets ($\tau\approx6700$/$6610$,
$R^2=0.9999$), so the collapse is gauge absorption, not an undecodable supervised
axis.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}{%
\textbf{Proposition (absorbability criterion).} Let a channel-wise affine $(\gamma,\beta)$ act on an activation immediately consumed by a learned linear map $A$ with optional bias $b$. The \emph{scale} is always gauge, $A(\gamma\odot x)=(A\diag(\gamma))\,x$, absorbed into $A$ with or without a bias. The \emph{shift} is gauge only when $A$ carries a trainable bias and no nonlinearity intervenes, $A(x+\beta)+b=Ax+(b+A\beta)$; without such a bias it is non-absorbable. A mechanism that instead modifies the in-kernel scan parameters $W_\Delta,W_B,W_C$---or any quantity strictly between a block's bounding projections---is \emph{not} gauge-absorbable. \textit{Corollary:} in the Mamba3 configuration used here (bias-free in/out projections; a lone $dt$ bias that can absorb only the shift's $\Delta$-component), input- and output-side FiLM share the same split: scale is gauge ($W_{\{\Delta,B,C\}}\diag(\gamma)$ or $\diag(\gamma)W_{\text{out}}$), while shift decays only as an unrewarded non-absorbable component.%
}}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Constant vs.\ input-dependent components.}
The criterion applies to the \emph{constant} affine component induced when the
router is uniform. A trained FiLM can also have an input-dependent component through
the router. In weakly decodable regimes the supervised router collapses toward
uniform, so the modulation reduces to a constant scale plus shift; the scale is
gauge-absorbable and the shift is unrewarded. When the regime is strongly decodable,
the input-dependent component can survive, as the LM control below illustrates.

\paragraph{Why identity is a free direction: gauge absorption.}
By the criterion, our input-side affine $\tilde{x}=\gamma\odot\hat{x}+\beta$ is
consumed immediately by the re-projections $\Delta_t=\sigma(W_\Delta\tilde{x}_t)$,
$\mathbf{B}_t=W_B\tilde{x}_t$, $\mathbf{C}_t=W_C\tilde{x}_t$. Its scale folds into
$W_{\{\Delta,B,C\}}$ with \textbf{no change to the function the block computes}, so
the host weights can represent any constant scale themselves. The shift would fold
only in a biased projection; in the Mamba3 blocks used here (a lone $dt$ bias can
absorb only its $\Delta$-component), it is instead an
additive component that the weakly decodable regime objective does not reward. Joint
training therefore folds the scale into the host weights and decays the residual
shift, returning $(\gamma,\beta)\to(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0})$, which is precisely the
monotone decay we observe. The similar order-of-thousands decay fits follow because
the absorption direction is a property of the block parameterization, not the data.
The actionable negative is that the most kernel-friendly, drop-in form of the idea
is exactly the form the optimizer can and does undo when the router is uniform.

\paragraph{An output-side placement: the same split.}
The gauge argument names an obvious next test, gating \emph{after} the scan. The
output scale folds into the bias-free $W_{\text{out}}$
($\gamma\odot(W_{\text{out}}h)=(\diag(\gamma)W_{\text{out}})h$), while the shift,
with no output bias and a nonlinear RMSNorm before the next block, is genuinely
\textbf{non-absorbable}. The experiment bears this out. Under the identical
forced-active escalation, the output-side $\mathrm{film}_g$ and $\mathrm{film}_b$
decay in lockstep on both datasets ($\mathrm{film}_g$: $0.250\to0.146$ FI-2010,
$0.250\to0.143$ Kaggle) with the same order-of-thousands descriptive fits
($\tau\approx7065$ FI-2010 / $6598$ Kaggle, $R^2>0.999$), and input- and
output-side endpoints differ only at plotting precision. The symmetry between
placements is therefore scale gauge plus unrewarded shift: the host weights absorb
the scale on either face, and the non-absorbable shift decays because the weakly
decodable regime loss does not reward it. A modulation escaping both must act
\emph{between} the block's bounding projections, modulating $W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$
inside the fused kernel.

\paragraph{The boundary, demonstrated (with a gauge-vs-regularization control).}
Toy-scale experiments turn the design rule from asserted to demonstrated.
\emph{(i) Folding.} On a toy reference scan with biased projections, an
input-affine modulation folds into the host projections to floating-point precision
(fold residual $1.2\times10^{-6}$) and decays to identity
($\mathrm{film}_g: 0.086\to0.010$). The \emph{same} modulation applied to
$\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ \emph{inside} the scan is non-absorbable
($1.3\times10^{-2}$, $\sim10^4\times$ larger) and \textbf{persists}
($0.093\to0.175$, seeds 0--3), so the in-kernel target the rule names is real at
reference-scan scale. In the Mamba3 runs (bias-free in/out projections), this fold
validates the scale gauge; the additive shift is handled by the unrewarded-shift
argument above.
\emph{(ii) Weight-decay control.} Removing weight decay flattens the decay ($\tau$
from $\approx6685$ to $\approx3\times10^5$, $R^2$ $0.9999\!\to\!0.48$), so the
gauge-direction decay is driven by \textbf{weight decay over a flat loss}, not the
objective. It is absent for the in-kernel placement in this toy setting; a full
world-model or foundation-model in-kernel implementation remains future work.

\paragraph{When conditioning \emph{does} survive: regime decodability.}
The collapse is not universal, and the boundary is itself actionable. A Mamba
char-LM control on a two-domain corpus (English prose vs.\ Python source), with the
regime inferred by a context-aware router supervised on the domain and the
\emph{same} input-affine FiLM, does \textbf{not} collapse. In this single-seed toy
control, a strongly-decodable regime (router accuracy $\ge0.9$; $0.91$ at the last
logged step) makes
$\mathrm{film}_g$ \emph{rise} from $0.11$ to $0.52$; the qualitative boundary is the
point, not the calibrated plateau value. Gauge absorption always removes the
\emph{constant scale} component. The
\emph{input-dependent} part survives only when the regime is decodable enough to be
worth encoding. In the LOB settings the regime is weakly decodable, the router
collapses to uniform, and the modulation reduces to a constant scale plus an
unrewarded shift.
\textbf{The actionable rule, refined:} skip the input-affine FiLM exactly when the
regime is weakly decodable from the block input; if it is strongly and contextually
decodable, the affine \emph{can} engage.


\subsection{Architecture-agnosticism check (matched-parameter backbone ablation)}\label{sec:ablation}

We compare Mamba-1, Mamba-2, Mamba-3 (SISO), and a Transformer-over-time at matched
budgets ($\sim10.3$M) on FI-2010 and the Kaggle BTC spot book, reporting held-out

Transformer matched to the Mamba-3 block ($9.61$M) is likewise competitive but
non-dominant, so the non-dominance is not an under-tuned-baseline artifact. These
single-seed runs show no clear dominant backbone in this setup; they are not a

\emph{Why SISO.} We run Mamba-3 in SISO throughout. The MIMO kernel's dynamic

rule names. The surviving, in-kernel modulation of $\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$
lives behind the same fused-kernel wall.

\paragraph{Deflated economic coda (substrate).}\label{sec:coda}
The substrate yields a positive result the architecture did \emph{not} carry. The
world model's gated settlement probability is a survivable edge on the
high-predictability settlement subset (market-block 95\% CI
$[+\$2{,}445,\,+\$7{,}747]$ over $127$ traded/evaluable markets), yet it is
\textbf{architecture-independent}. On the full $645$-market held-out BTC set, the
settlement head totals $+\$5{,}147$; a logistic on the \emph{same} features exceeds
it ($+\$6{,}914$, $243$ traded/evaluable markets under the same gate). The operative
variable is the features and the gate, \textbf{not the sequence model}; the
$246$-market GBT comparison is in Appendix~\ref{app:coda}.

\section{Discussion: What a Practitioner Should Do Differently}\label{sec:discussion}

The interpretability finding is small: the scale component of an input-affine on a
Mamba block input is a gauge direction. The lift-and-use protocol is:

\begin{shaded}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Diagnostic protocol: will my selective-scan conditioning
survive training?}\\[2pt]
\textbf{1. Instrument the gate.} Log $\mathrm{film}_g=\mathrm{mean}\,|\gamma-1|$ and
$\mathrm{reg}_H$. Before crediting conditioning, require $\mathrm{film}_g>0$ and
$\mathrm{reg}_H<\log R$; a positive gap against an inert gate is a distributional
artifact, not a conditioning effect.\\[2pt]
\textbf{2. Run the fold-residual placement test.} On a tiny reference scan, fit the
host projections to absorb the forced-active scale (and any shift only when the host
projection has a trainable bias). Input- and output-affine scales fold to round-off
($\sim\!10^{-6}$); a placement survives only if its residual is large
($\sim\!10^{-2}$), i.e.\ it acts on $\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ between the
block's bounding projections.\\[2pt]
\textbf{3. Check regime decodability.} Affine conditioning engages only when the
regime is strongly decodable from the block input. If the router collapses to
uniform, only a constant scale-plus-shift remains; the scale is gauge-absorbable and
the shift is unrewarded, so skip the affine. (Established on a single-seed LM
control, so treat the threshold as directional, not calibrated.)
\end{shaded}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The forced-active decay is monotone over our training horizon and admits
order-of-thousands descriptive fits with asymptotes at or below identity, but the
within-horizon endpoint is still above identity; we do not rule out every positive
plateau under every optimizer/schedule. The LM control is single-seed and the
in-kernel toy is seeds 0--3, while the market null is multi-setting and multi-seed.
The economic coda is a backtest substrate, not part of the interpretability claim.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

An input-affine FiLM on a Mamba block input contains a \textbf{scale gauge}; its
shift decays when the weakly decodable regime loss does not reward it. In our runs
the affine silently returns to identity, and output-side placement shows the same
split. The practitioner's rule is the absorbability criterion: condition between a
block's bounding projections, on $\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ inside the fused
kernel (validated at toy scale, seeds 0--3), or gate every claim on the two-number
$\mathrm{film}_g/\mathrm{reg}_H$ check.

\paragraph{Economic-coda caveats (detail).}
The edge harvests the lag between the underlying spot price and the contract book.
Our backtest assumes each order executes at the prices recorded in the book, and
that the resting liquidity there is available to trade against; in live trading,
large orders would move the price against us and erode some of the edge. Only the train-frozen predictability gate is
\emph{confirmatory}; the depth-band and early-market cuts are \emph{exploratory},
subject to forking-paths caveats. The reliability table and PnL totals are
recorded-vintage structural diagnostics. The over-confidence pattern is structural,
not vintage-specific.

\paragraph{Regime robustness via a tradeable-EV head (detail).}
We also supervise the \emph{tradeable} expected value directly, with a
\texttt{BookRelativeEdgeHead} trained under Huber loss on
$e_{\text{yes}}=\text{outcome}-\text{ask}_{\text{yes}}$ and
$e_{\text{no}}=(1-\text{outcome})-\text{ask}_{\text{no}}$, where
$\text{ask}_{\text{yes}}$ and $\text{ask}_{\text{no}}$ are the prices to buy the YES
and NO contracts, so the target is near zero (abstain) when the book is already
fair. This makes the low-predictability
regime robust: low-pred $+\$2{,}368$ at P(profit)$=0.99$ (seed 0), and a three-seed
mean of $+\$4{,}779$ total at mean low-pred P(profit)$=0.98$, collapsing the
high-vs-low PnL spread $6.2\times$ ($\$5{,}044\to\$814$) at total PnL comparable to
settlement ($+\$5{,}147$). The same-feature logistic is \emph{already} regime-robust
(high-pred $+\$2{,}448$, low-pred $+\$4{,}466$, both P(profit)$\ge0.99$), so the
low-predictability signal is not intrinsically hard. The EV head is a real fix
\emph{for the world-model readout}, not the only way to capture it.

\section{Linear-probe localization of regime decodability}\label{app:probe}

Two probes localize the failure behind the null. A post-hoc reading of the
jointly-supervised router shows it is uniform \emph{per step} (per-sample entropy
$\approx\log 4$) with near-chance agreement against the efficiency-ratio bucket
($0.25$--$0.31$ vs.\ $0.25$). Direct supervision does not even make the router
regime-discriminative. A frozen linear probe on the unmodulated features decodes
the per-step predictability bucket only weakly ($0.33$--$0.36$ vs.\ $0.25$ chance)
and the volatility bucket somewhat better ($0.43$--$0.47$). Yet the volatility-axis
gap is \emph{also} null. So even on the axis the representation encodes best, and
even when the regime is supplied by supervision, the in-scan modulation collapses to
identity. (The degenerate next-tick direction head is de-confounded by
inverse-frequency class balancing, which yields a non-degenerate macro-F1 with the
gap still null; the single borderline gap, Kaggle spot-vol at $n{=}2$, is disproved
by a pre-registered third seed that flips its sign.)
