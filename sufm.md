## Abstract

Selective state-space models (Mamba) have reshaped long-context sequence modeling, yet *which* forms of conditioning survive their training dynamics is poorly understood. We study FiLM-style regime conditioning of a Mamba backbone — a per-block, channel-wise affine on each block's input, meant to steer the input-dependent selective-scan parameters $\Delta, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}$ — and find it a **decisive null**: under joint training the modulation collapses to identity and cannot be forced active even by direct supervision. We explain this mechanistically. An input-side affine is a *gauge* direction that folds losslessly into the block's own $\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ projections, so the optimizer slides it back to identity. The signature is dataset- and architecture-invariant: forced-active, efficiency-ratio-supervised escalations decay to identity with a shared time constant ($\tau \approx 7000$ steps, $R^2 > 0.999$) across three very different distributions — FI-2010 equities, Kaggle crypto spot books, and Polymarket prediction markets — implicating the optimizer, not the data. We then sharpen the mechanism with an output-side placement that gates each block's output instead of its input. Its multiplicative scale merely folds into the block's *other* (bias-free output) projection and decays with the same constant ($\tau \approx 7000$ on both datasets, $R^2 > 0.999$), while the one genuinely non-absorbable component — an additive shift — decays in lockstep. The null is thus **overdetermined**: gauge absorption removes every direction the host weights can represent, and reconstruction dominance removes the one they cannot. Escaping both would require modulating the scan parameters *inside* the fused kernel, which our hardware cannot run. We demonstrate this boundary at toy scale on the reference scan — an in-kernel modulation is non-absorbable and survives the training that drives the input-affine form to identity — and bound the null with a language-model control: a Mamba char-LM on a strongly-decodable regime (code vs. prose) *does* engage the modulation, so the collapse is specifically the fate of *weakly-decodable*, drop-in regime conditioning. The financial datasets are a demonstration substrate, not the subject; consistent with a result about how selective scans *train* rather than which one is best, a matched-parameter ablation (Mamba-1/2/3 and a Mamba-3-matched Transformer) shows no backbone dominates, and the one economic signal we do find is reproduced by a logistic regression on the same features — architecture-independent, as the null predicts.

## 1. Introduction

A practitioner who wants a sequence model to behave differently in different regimes will reach for the most natural tool available: condition the model on an inferred regime. For a Mamba backbone the most kernel-friendly way to do this is a FiLM-style affine on each block's input, because the selective scan's parameters $\Delta_t, \mathbf{B}_t, \mathbf{C}_t$ are themselves input-dependent (§3) — so an affine on the input propagates a regime signal *into* the dynamics without touching the fused CUDA kernel. It is the drop-in form of the idea. **We find that it does not survive training, and we explain why.**

We attach an exactly zero-initialized FiLM modulator (a regime router plus a hypernetwork emitting per-block $\gamma, \beta$) to a Mamba-3 world model and train it jointly with the host objective. Despite the cleanest possible control — at initialization the modulated backbone is *bit-identical* to the unmodulated one — and despite the strongest activation pressure we can apply (forced-active initialization plus direct supervision of the router on a regime axis), the modulation **decays monotonically back to identity** on every objective and dataset we test. This is the paper's spine, and it is a statement about *training dynamics*, not about expressivity: a *frozen* router probes to 0.92 agreement with the regime bucket, so the capacity exists; joint training will not use it.

The explanation is a **gauge symmetry of the block parameterization**. An input-side channel-wise scale folds *exactly* into the block's input projections as a right-multiplied diagonal $W_\bullet\,\mathrm{diag}(\gamma)$, and the shift folds into their biases, with no change to the function the block computes. Identity is therefore a flat, lossless valley — a gauge direction — and the optimizer slides along it, returning $(\gamma, \beta)$ to $(\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{0})$. This predicts, and we observe, a decay signature that is a property of the *block*, not the data: a forced-active escalation decays with the **same time constant $\tau \approx 7000$ steps ($R^2 > 0.999$)** on FI-2010 equities and Kaggle crypto — two markets with nothing in common but the optimizer that trains them.

We then run the test the gauge argument names. An **output-side** placement gates the block's output rather than its input; one might expect it to escape absorption. It does not. Each Mamba-3 block ends in a *bias-free* output projection $W_{\text{out}}$, so the output scale folds into *that* instead — the same gauge move on the block's other face, with the same $\tau$. The output shift $\beta$ is genuinely non-absorbable (no output bias, a nonlinear RMSNorm separating it from the next block) — and it decays in lockstep anyway, killed by reconstruction dominance because it earns nothing under the objective. The null is therefore **overdetermined**: two independent mechanisms return every component to identity, and the only escape route left is *inside* the fused selective-scan kernel — exactly the kernel-level change our hardware cannot run.

**A methodological hazard falls out of this.** The metric one would naively read — an in-regime/out-of-regime generalization gap — reads *positive*, sometimes with the wrong sign, while the modulation is provably inert; the sign even flips with head calibration. The FiLM-activation diagnostics ($\mathrm{film}_g = \mathrm{mean}\,|\gamma - 1|$, router entropy $\mathrm{reg}_H$) are therefore **load-bearing** for any regime-conditioning claim: without them, a null mechanism is easily mistaken for a positive effect.

**Contributions.**
1. A **decisive, mechanistically explained null** for input-affine FiLM conditioning of a selective scan: the modulation collapses to identity under joint training and cannot be forced active, across five objectives and three LOB distributions (§4).
2. The mechanism — **gauge absorption** — together with its dataset- and architecture-invariant decay signature ($\tau \approx 7000$, $R^2 > 0.999$), and an **output-side placement that makes the null overdetermined** (gauge absorption + reconstruction dominance), delineating the boundary as a location *inside the kernel* rather than a parameterization that survives. The mechanism is **demonstrated**, not just argued: a toy reference-scan experiment shows the in-kernel placement is non-absorbable and survives while the input-affine form decays, and a no-weight-decay ablation shows the gauge decay is **weight-decay-driven over a flat loss** (§5).
3. A **boundary at regime decodability**: a Mamba language-model control (prose vs. code) shows that a *strongly*-decodable regime engages the modulation, so the optimizer-driven collapse is the fate of *weakly*-decodable conditioning specifically — scoping the dataset-invariance claim honestly (§5).
4. A **load-bearing-diagnostic hazard**: the generalization-gap metric reads positive for reasons unrelated to the mechanism under test (§4–§5).
5. A **demonstration substrate** across three LOB datasets with an **architecture-agnosticism check** (no backbone dominates), and a deflated economic coda showing the one signal we find is **architecture-independent** — a logistic regression on the same features reproduces it (§6). This is exactly what a result about how selective scans *train*, not which is best, should predict.

The financial machinery (a Mamba-3 world model in the DreamerV3/Drama lineage) is the stress-substrate that let us probe the mechanism on genuinely different data; it is described only to the depth the null requires.

## 2. Related Work

**Selective state-space models and Mamba.** S4, S5, and Mamba give $O(n)$-complexity alternatives to attention (Gu et al. 2022; Smith et al. 2023; Gu & Dao 2024). Mamba (Gu & Dao 2024) introduced *selective* state spaces in which $\Delta$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are input-dependent, enabling content-aware filtering; Mamba-2 (Dao & Gu 2024) cast this as structured masked attention; Mamba-3 (Lahoti et al. 2026) adds MIMO state transitions. Our object of study is not a new SSM but **how a selective scan trains under an added affine** — specifically, that the very input-dependence that makes the modulation seem natural is what makes it gauge-absorbable.

**FiLM and conditioning.** Feature-wise linear modulation (Perez et al. 2018) injects context via per-channel affine parameters and is a default conditioning primitive across vision and multi-task learning. We are not aware of prior work that asks whether a FiLM affine *persists* under joint training of a selective scan, or that identifies its absorption into the host projections as a gauge direction.

**World models.** DreamerV3 (Hafner et al. 2023) established the categorical-latent RSSM template; Drama (Wang et al. 2024) replaced the RSSM recurrence with a Mamba-2 selective scan. We instantiate this lineage with a Mamba-3 backbone purely as a substrate; no result in our spine depends on the world-model framing beyond its providing a reconstruction objective and three distinct data distributions.

**LOB / regime modeling (substrate context).** DeepLOB (Zhang et al. 2019) and the FI-2010 benchmark (Ntakaris et al. 2018) anchor LOB direction prediction; the LOBCAST benchmark (Prata et al. 2023) documents the in-distribution macro-F1 range we use to confirm our data carries genuine signal. Classical regime-switching (Hamilton 1989) and changepoint detection (Adams & MacKay 2007) motivate having a regime axis at all. We use the **efficiency-ratio** predictability axis (Kaufman) as the most defensible regime signal in the study.

## 3. Background: the selective scan and the FiLM modulator

**Selective state-space model.** A discretized SSM maps $x_t$ to $y_t$ through a latent state, $h_t = \bar{\mathbf{A}} h_{t-1} + \bar{\mathbf{B}} x_t$, $y_t = \mathbf{C} h_t$. Mamba makes the scan parameters **input-dependent** via learned linear projections of the block input $x_t$:

$$\Delta_t = \sigma(W_\Delta\,x_t), \qquad \mathbf{B}_t = W_B\,x_t, \qquad \mathbf{C}_t = W_C\,x_t.$$

This selection mechanism is exactly the property a regime-conditioner would exploit: *any* affine transformation of $x_t$ propagates into all three scan parameters. It is also, as §5 shows, exactly the property that makes an input affine absorbable.

**RegimeFiLM modulator.** A regime head infers a soft categorical distribution over $R$ regimes from the stem output and reads out a low-dimensional regime embedding $r_t$; a hypernetwork maps $r_t$ to per-block FiLM parameters, bounded for bf16 stability:

$$\bm{\alpha}_t = \mathrm{softmax}(W_r\,x_t^{(0)}) \in \mathbb{R}^R, \quad r_t = \bm{\alpha}_t^\top E, \quad \gamma^{(\ell)} = 1 + \tanh(\tilde\gamma^{(\ell)}),\ \ \beta^{(\ell)} = \tanh(\tilde\beta^{(\ell)}).$$

The hypernetwork is initialized to zero, so $\gamma^{(\ell)} = \mathbf{1}$ and $\beta^{(\ell)} = \mathbf{0}$ at step 0 — the modulated backbone is **exactly the unmodulated baseline at initialization**, an exact paired control. A Switch-Transformer-style load-balance term maximizes the entropy of the *batch-averaged* router distribution, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{regime}} = \lambda_{\text{ent}}(\log R - H[\bar{\bm\alpha}])$, regularizing marginal regime usage while leaving per-step assignments free to be peaky. (We ablate this term away in the strongest-null test, below, so the observed router uniformity is not attributable to it.)

**Two application sites.** The **input-side** placement routes the regime into $\Delta_t, \mathbf{B}_t, \mathbf{C}_t$ through the selection mechanism:

$$x_t^{(\ell)} = x_t^{(\ell-1)} + \mathrm{Mamba3}\!\left(\gamma^{(\ell)} \odot \mathrm{RMSNorm}(x_t^{(\ell-1)}) + \beta^{(\ell)}\right). \tag{input-side}$$

As a control we also expose a **post-scan** site that gates the block *output*, sharing the identical modulator and differing only in where the affine lands:

$$x_t^{(\ell)} = x_t^{(\ell-1)} + \gamma^{(\ell)} \odot \mathrm{Mamba3}\!\left(\mathrm{RMSNorm}(x_t^{(\ell-1)})\right) + \beta^{(\ell)}. \tag{post-scan}$$

The input-side scale folds into $W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$; the post-scan scale folds into the bias-free output projection $W_{\text{out}}$; only the post-scan additive shift is non-absorbable (§5).

**Diagnostics.** We gate every claim on two activation diagnostics: $\mathrm{film}_g = \mathrm{mean}\,|\gamma - 1|$ (zero at identity) and the router entropy $\mathrm{reg}_H$ (whose uniform maximum is $\log R$). A positive generalization gap with an inert FiLM is an artifact, not a regime-conditioning effect.

## 4. RegimeFiLM is a decisive null

**The result.** Across five objectives — Student-$t$ NLL (volatility/scale), volume NLL (order-flow), direction macro-F1, Polymarket settlement BCE, and a simulated-trading PnL objective — FiLM collapses to identity under joint training. In every family, on both datasets and on a money objective, $\mathrm{film}_g$ decays toward zero and the router entropy $\mathrm{reg}_H$ sits at its uniform maximum ($\log R$: $\log 4$ on FI-2010, $\log 8$ on Polymarket). Where a generalization gap reads positive (direction $+0.008$, settlement $+0.18$, by sign), FiLM is provably inert, the treatment is *absolutely worse* in both regimes, and the gap is a high$-$low distributional artifact whose sign flips with head calibration — which is exactly why the activation diagnostics are load-bearing.

## 5. Mechanism: gauge absorption, and an overdetermined null

**Why identity is a free direction.** The decay has a simple mechanistic explanation that also *bounds* the claim. The input-side modulation $\tilde{x} = \gamma \odot \hat{x} + \beta$ is applied to a block input that the very next operations re-project: $\Delta_t = \sigma(W_\Delta \tilde{x}_t)$, $\mathbf{B}_t = W_B \tilde{x}_t$, $\mathbf{C}_t = W_C \tilde{x}_t$. A constant channel-wise scale folds **exactly** into those projections as a right-multiplied diagonal $W_\bullet\,\mathrm{diag}(\gamma)$, and the shift folds into their biases, with **no change to the function the block computes**; the host weights can thus represent any constant $(\gamma, \beta)$ themselves. Identity is therefore a **gauge** direction — a flat, lossless valley the optimizer can slide along — so joint training folds the modulation into the host weights and returns $(\gamma, \beta)$ to $(\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{0})$, which is precisely the monotone decay we observe.

This sharpens the result two ways. First, it explains the **dataset-invariant decay constant** (Figure 2): the absorption direction is a property of the block parameterization, not of the data. Second, it **scopes** the null: the claim is that *input-affine* conditioning of a selective scan is gauge-absorbable and will not persist under joint training — not that regime conditioning is impossible in principle. Mechanisms outside this gauge — modulating $W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$ directly *inside* the kernel, or conditioning a quantity inserted *between* the block's bounding projections — are not trivially absorbable and are the natural next test. Our contribution is the negative that matters in practice: the most kernel-friendly, drop-in form of the idea, the one that needs no change to the fused CUDA kernel, is exactly the form the optimizer can and does undo.

**An output-side placement: the null is overdetermined.** The gauge argument names an obvious next test — gating *after* the scan — so we run it, under the *identical* forced-active, ER-supervised escalation, changing only where the learned $(\gamma, \beta)$ are applied. Instead of the input-side $\tilde{x} = \gamma \odot \hat{x} + \beta$, we gate the block output $y' = \gamma \odot y + \beta$. This placement does **not** escape absorption, and the reason is instructive. Each Mamba-3 block ends in a **bias-free** linear output projection $y = W_{\text{out}}\,h$, so a channel-wise output scale folds exactly into it, $\gamma \odot (W_{\text{out}} h) = (\mathrm{diag}(\gamma)\,W_{\text{out}})\,h$ — the same gauge move as the input side, now on the block's *other* face. The additive shift $\beta$ is different: with no output bias to absorb it and a nonlinear RMSNorm separating it from the next block's projections, $\beta$ is genuinely **non-absorbable**.

The escalation resolves both at once. Under forced activation and direct ER supervision, $\mathrm{film}_g$ *and* $\mathrm{film}_b$ decay monotonically and in lockstep on both datasets ($\mathrm{film}_g: 0.250 \to 0.147$ FI-2010, $0.250 \to 0.143$ Kaggle; $\mathrm{film}_b$ tracks it within $0.005$), with $\mathrm{reg}_H \approx \log 4$ throughout. The exponential fit gives the **same** dataset-invariant signature as the input-side gate — $a \approx 0.30$, $\tau \approx 7065$ (FI-2010) / $6598$ (Kaggle), asymptote $c \approx -0.05$, $R^2 > 0.999$ — so the modulation provably converges to identity here too.

The null is therefore **overdetermined by two independent mechanisms**:
- **Gauge absorption** disposes of every direction the host weights can represent — the multiplicative scale on *either* face of the block — which is why the decay constant is invariant to placement.
- **Reconstruction dominance** disposes of the one direction they cannot: the non-absorbable shift $\beta$, returned to identity at the same rate purely because it earns nothing under the reconstruction objective.

A modulation escaping both would have to act *between* the block's bounding projections — modulating $W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$ within the fused selective-scan kernel — which is precisely the kernel-level change outside our hardware envelope (§6). We thus delineate the boundary not as a parameterization that survives training, but as the location — inside the kernel — a surviving modulation would have to occupy.

**The boundary, demonstrated.** We make this concrete at toy scale on the pure-PyTorch *reference* scan (the slow path `mamba_ssm` ships, which the fused-kernel hardware limit does not touch). An input-affine modulation folds into the host projections to floating-point precision (max output deviation $1.2\times10^{-6}$) and **decays to identity** under joint training with weight decay ($\mathrm{film}_g: 0.086 \to 0.010$), while the *same* modulation applied to the projected $\Delta/\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{C}$ inside the scan is provably non-absorbable (best-fit fold residual $1.3\times10^{-2}$, $\sim 10^4\times$ larger) and **persists** under identical training ($\mathrm{film}_g: 0.093 \to 0.175$, seeds 0–3) — a modulation that lives inside the kernel and survives. The same distinction holds on the *full* model: rerunning the forced-active, ER-supervised escalation with weight decay removed flattens the decay — the exponential time constant lengthens from $\tau \approx 6685$ ($R^2 = 0.9999$) to $\tau \approx 3\times10^5$ ($R^2 = 0.48$, i.e. no clean decay), with $\mathrm{film}_g$ left at its forced-active initialization — and the toy reproduces the same flattening (input-affine $\mathrm{film}_g: 0.086 \to 0.342$ with weight decay off vs. $0.086 \to 0.010$ with it on). The decay along the gauge direction is therefore **driven by weight decay over a flat loss**, not by the objective, distinguishing gauge absorption from a modulation that merely earns nothing. The boundary is thus demonstrated, not merely asserted: the in-kernel placement is non-absorbable, and a modulation there survives the very training that drives the input-affine form to identity.

**The regime is decodable yet unused — and not even needed.** Two probes localize the failure. A post-hoc reading of the jointly-supervised router shows it is uniform *per step* (per-sample entropy $\approx \log 4$) with near-chance agreement against the efficiency-ratio bucket ($0.25$–$0.31$ vs. $0.25$): direct supervision does not even make the router regime-discriminative. A frozen linear probe on the unmodulated features decodes the per-step predictability bucket only weakly ($0.33$–$0.36$ vs. $0.25$ chance) and the volatility bucket somewhat better ($0.43$–$0.47$) — yet the volatility-axis gap is *also* null. So the null does not hinge on the regime being under-represented: even on the axis the representation encodes best, and even when the regime is supplied by supervision, the in-scan modulation collapses to identity.

**Language-model control: the null is bounded by regime decodability.** Because the collapse is optimizer-driven, the natural worry is that it is nonetheless specific to LOB data; a minimal language-model control maps where the boundary lies. We train a 2-layer char-level Mamba LM (built directly on `mamba_ssm` blocks) on a two-domain corpus — English prose vs. Python source — with the per-position regime inferred by a context-aware router supervised on the domain and the *same* input-affine FiLM gating the second block. Here the regime is *strongly* decodable: the router reaches $\sim 0.95$ domain accuracy, and the FiLM does **not** collapse — $\mathrm{film}_g$ rises from $0.11$ to $0.52$ and plateaus, the opposite of the LOB decay. This sharpens the mechanism rather than counters it. Gauge absorption removes the *constant* component of the affine unconditionally (the folding test above); the *input-dependent* component survives only when the regime is decodable enough to be worth encoding. In every LOB setting the predictability/volatility regime is weakly decodable and the router collapses to uniform ($\mathrm{reg}_H = \log R$), so the modulation reduces to its gauge-absorbable constant part and decays; a strongly-decodable regime engages it. We therefore scope the claim accordingly: the optimizer-driven collapse holds for **weakly-decodable** regime conditioning of selective scans — the predictability and volatility axes a financial world model actually faces — not for all conditioning.

**The gap is real, and FiLM closes none of it.** The null is not an absence of anything to fix. On the Kaggle spot book, full-channel reconstruction NLL is $+0.105$ worse on the high-volatility (shifted) windows than on the low-volatility (reference) windows, in *both* arms. RegimeFiLM is designed precisely to absorb such shifts by conditioning the dynamics on the inferred regime, yet it absorbs essentially none of it — the treatment narrows the degradation by $\sim 0.001$ (a $\sim 1\%$ sliver) while $\mathrm{film}_g$ is provably inert. The architecture is not failing for want of a gap to close; it is failing to engage a gap that is plainly present.

**The load-bearing-diagnostic hazard, restated.** Because a positive generalization gap co-occurred with an inert FiLM in multiple families — and because its sign flips with head calibration — a study that reported only the gap would have concluded "thesis supported" while the mechanism under test did nothing. The activation diagnostics are what convert an apparent positive into a correctly-read null. We surface this as a transferable lesson for regime-conditioning work, not as a detail of our setup.

## 6. Demonstration substrate and architecture-agnosticism

The mechanism above is the subject. The financial machinery is the **substrate** that let us probe it on three genuinely different distributions; we compress it to what the null and its agnosticism check require.

**Substrate.** We build a Mamba-3 world model for LOB microstructure in the DreamerV3/Drama lineage: a Transformer-over-depth encoder (each book level a token, plus binary-market invariants — boundary distance, logit-space velocity, Amihud illiquidity, variance ratio), feeding a stack of Mamba-3 SISO blocks that drive a categorical-latent dynamics prior with a reconstruction objective. The system carries direction, Hawkes-intensity, settlement, and book-relative-EV auxiliary heads, each gated by a flag; only the settlement and EV heads bear on any result below. Episodic memory, the five Lopez de Prado bar types, the POMDP/imagination-RL formalism, and the EV-head architectural details are implemented but off the spine and not exercised by any spine result. For the Polymarket substrate we rebuild the feature pipeline to expose the **spot path** that *defines* settlement (the underlying Chainlink/Binance close vs. open reference), a time-to-expiry clock, an honest TTE-weighted settlement target, cross-interval context, and an asset embedding — the causal signal of which the settlement label is a function.

**Backbone ablation (no backbone dominates).** Under matched parameter budgets ($\sim 10.3$M; actuals below), seed 0, 3000 steps, on FI-2010 and the Kaggle BTC spot book, we report held-out full-channel Student-$t$ reconstruction NLL (lower is better). The result is a genuine ambiguity, not a clean win: **no backbone leads on both datasets**. A pre-norm Transformer matched to the Mamba-3 block (RMSNorm, SiLU, $4\times$ FFN; $9.61$M params) is likewise competitive but non-dominant ($-0.5041$ FI-2010, $0.0224$ Kaggle), so the non-dominance is not an artifact of an under-tuned post-norm baseline. This is exactly what a result about how selective scans *train*, rather than which one is best, should predict.

**The economic coda is architecture-independent (deflated).** Given the spot path, the model's over-confident-but-directional settlement probability — traded by a selective-participation strategy that bets only when a train-frozen causal predictability (efficiency-ratio) gate fires, against a mandatory no-model naive control and summarized by a **market-block** bootstrap that resamples whole markets (so a market's correlated trades count once) — is a survivable, naive-beating edge **in the regime the gate targets**: across $127$ independent held-out markets it holds P(profit)$=1.0$ with a 95% CI of $[+\$2{,}445,\,+\$7{,}747]$. Crucially, the edge is **architecture-independent**: a **logistic regression on the same spot-conditioned features reproduces and exceeds it ($+\$6{,}914$, Brier $0.146$**, vs. the world-model settlement head's $+\$5{,}147$ at Brier $0.206$, and a 3-seed EV-head mean of $+\$4{,}779$), surviving the same market-block bootstrap (P$=1.0$ over $243$ independent markets). Binary-contract settlement is a near-deterministic function of the spot move since open, and a linear readout captures it at least as cleanly as the sequence model — so what carries the edge is the spot-conditioned features and the selective-participation framing, **not** the backbone. This *confirms* the null's lesson from the other side: conditioning the dynamics did not help, and the one thing that did is not the architecture either. (The settlement head's low-predictability regime is marginal — $+\$51$, P$=0.63$ — and is made robust by a direct expected-value head that supervises tradeable EV net of the spread, lifting P to $0.98$–$0.99$ and collapsing the high-vs-low PnL spread $6.2\times$ at comparable total PnL; full tables in the long-form version.)

**Honesty / caveats.** The economic gates are split by evidential status: only the predictability gate is **confirmatory** (its threshold is fit on the training split and frozen before any held-out evaluation); the depth-band and early-market cuts are **exploratory** post-hoc characterizations subject to the usual forking-paths caveat. The settlement-head reliability diagnostic (below) is a **recorded-vintage structural diagnostic** — the over-confidence pattern is structural, not vintage-specific — and is labeled as such. All numbers above are on a single verified data vintage and are reported, not recomputed.

## 7. Conclusion

We studied the most natural, most kernel-friendly way to make a Mamba backbone regime-aware — an input-affine FiLM on each block — and found it a **decisive training-dynamics null**: the modulation collapses to identity under joint training and cannot be forced active even by direct supervision on the most defensible regime signal in the study, across five objectives and three LOB distributions. The cause is a **gauge symmetry of the block parameterization**: an input affine folds losslessly into the block's own $W_\Delta/W_B/W_C$ projections, so the optimizer slides it back to identity, with a dataset- and architecture-invariant decay signature ($\tau \approx 7000$, $R^2 > 0.999$) that implicates the optimizer rather than the data. An **output-side placement makes the null overdetermined** — the scale folds into the block's bias-free output projection (gauge again), and the non-absorbable shift dies anyway under reconstruction dominance — leaving the only escape route *inside* the fused kernel, which our hardware cannot run. A **load-bearing-diagnostic hazard** accompanies the null: the generalization-gap metric reads positive, sometimes with the wrong sign, while the mechanism is inert, so the activation diagnostics are essential to reading the result correctly. Consistent with a result about how selective scans *train* rather than which is best, no backbone dominates a matched-parameter ablation, and the one economic signal we find is architecture-independent — a logistic regression on the same features reproduces it. The practical upshot for foundation-model conditioning is concrete: a conditioning mechanism that lives in a gauge direction of its host's parameterization will be undone by joint training, no matter how it is initialized or supervised, and only a placement *between* a block's bounding projections can hope to persist.
